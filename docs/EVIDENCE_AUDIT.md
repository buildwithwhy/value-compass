# Evidence audit — milestone 1

What the existing dataset and interface claimed, where those claims were not supported,
and what has been changed. Counts are generated by `node scripts/build-evidence.mjs`, not
asserted by hand.

**Scope note.** This audit reviews *how* claims are represented. It is not a fact-check of
the underlying research: no source was re-read against its claim except where noted, and
nothing in the dataset has been re-scored. The dataset is **not** fully audited.

---

## The headline problem

The project's own rubric states the principle clearly: *"Missing data ≠ zero. 'We can't
see it' is different from 'we looked and it's bad'. This distinction is the integrity of
the whole tool."*

The dataset did not follow it. **Of 90 axis assessments, zero were recorded as `n/a`.**
Every gap in the evidence had been resolved into a number instead — and the numbers
skewed low, so absence of information was being displayed as below-average conduct.

Worse, it skewed unevenly. "Nothing published" became a **2** for a young Western startup
(Perplexity, Lovable, Cursor, ElevenLabs — all "little ESG disclosure") and a **1** for
Chinese labs and xAI (Moonshot, DeepSeek — "opaque; Chinese data-labour context with no
disclosure"). The same evidentiary state, scored a point apart depending on where the
company is.

---

## Findings

### 1. Scores whose rationale primarily says information is unavailable — 21 of 90

| Maker | Axis | Recorded | The whole rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moonshot | transparency, culture, labour | 1, 1, 1 | "opaque practices, similar to DeepSeek" · "Little public information; no ESG disclosure" · "Opaque; Chinese data-labour context with no disclosure" |
| Replit | transparency, culture, labour | 2, 2, 2 | "its AI agent stack is largely opaque; not FMTI-scored" · "little ESG disclosure" · "little direct data-labour" |
| DeepSeek | culture, labour | 1, 1 | "No ESG disclosure; quant-fund parent culture; little public information" · "Opaque; Chinese data-labour context with no disclosure" |
| Mistral | culture, labour | 2, 2 | "publishes little ESG detail" · "labeling supply chain not disclosed" |
| Perplexity | transparency, culture | 1, 2 | "limited disclosure on how it sources and ranks answers" · "no major culture scandals; little ESG disclosure" |
| Lovable, Cursor | transparency, culture | 1–2 | "minimal disclosure about its own stack" · "little public information" |
| Gamma | transparency | 1 | "minimal disclosure about its own stack" |
| Cohere, ElevenLabs, Midjourney | culture | 2 | "limited public ESG disclosure" |
| xAI | labour | 1 | "Minimal labour disclosure; rapid build-out with little visibility" |

A score of **1 = "below norm"** and **0 = "worst-in-class"**. Assigning either on the basis
of silence asserts poor conduct the record does not support.

**Fixed.** These 21 scores are no longer displayed. The compass shows a gap, the matrix
shows a dash, comparisons exclude them, and the maker page explains why and shows the
original recorded value for review. Nothing was deleted or re-scored.

### 2. Conclusions resting on geographic or institutional context — 5 of 90

- Perplexity, Lovable, Gamma, Cursor — labour scored **2** from "inherits upstream
  model-supply-chain labour" / "depends on frontier-model APIs whose upstream labour it
  does not control". That is a statement about the business model, not a finding about the
  company.
- Gamma — culture scored **2** from "Lean, profitable, small team".

Two more of this kind were caught by finding 1 instead: Mistral's labour and culture scores
lean on "EU context" and "stronger EU labour norms"; DeepSeek's and Moonshot's lean on
"Chinese data-labour context". Jurisdiction is not evidence about a company.

**Fixed.** These display an "Inferred" badge and a line naming what was reasoned from, and
they are excluded from best/lowest comparisons.

### 3. Claims without a relevant source — 67 of 90

Three distinct failures, all previously invisible because the UI rendered any URL in the
`sources` array as support:

- **40 assessments have no source at all.** Every `culture_esg` score outside the frontier
  labs, every `wealth_dispersion` score bar four, and every `public_sharing` score for the
  tool layer. Microsoft carries **four** unsourced scores including its 4/4 on wealth
  dispersion — the highest score in the dataset.
- **24 cite only sector background.** The LSE and Brookings articles on Global-South data
  labour hang off almost every labour score, including for makers whose note says nothing
  is known about them. They are real research about the industry; they say nothing about
  Gamma or Cursor. The same is true of the Windfall Clause article attached to Anthropic's
  public-sharing score.
- **A further 3 cite the wrong thing about the right company** (hand-checked, recorded in
  `SOURCE_MISMATCH` in the build script): DeepSeek's and Cohere's public-sharing scores
  cite funding-round coverage rather than the open-weight releases they rest on; Google
  DeepMind's culture score cites the Stanford transparency index to support a claim about
  Alphabet's sustainability reporting and dual-class voting.
- **Stanford FMTI links are attached to ten makers the index did not score** — cited, in
  several cases, by notes that themselves say "Not FMTI-scored".

**Fixed.** Sources are now split into "Sources about *maker*" and a collapsed "Background
reading — not about *maker*" with a line on each explaining why it does not support the
claim. An assessment with no entity source carries a "No source" badge everywhere it
appears.

### 4. Funding events conflated — announced, pending, contingent, completed

The graph drew one kind of line for every funder→maker relationship. Flattened together
were:

- **Amazon → OpenAI**: "up to $50B… $15B initial + $35B **contingent on an OpenAI
  IPO/listing**" — drawn identically to Amazon's completed ~$8B equity in Anthropic.
- **Tencent → DeepSeek**: a first external round "**near closing** as of June 2026", with
  the state-backed National AI Industry Fund. DeepSeek's capital profile listed
  `China-linked (reported state fund…)` as a present attribute, so a round that has not
  closed was triggering a live "sovereign capital" concern.
- **Alphabet → Anthropic**: "~14%, capped 15%, **no votes/board**" — drawn identically to
  Alphabet's outright ownership of DeepMind.
- **ASML → Mistral**: "~11%… **CFO on strategic committee**" — board presence, undrawn.
- **High-Flyer → DeepSeek**: ~89.5% control, undrawn as control.
- **Saudi PIF → xAI**: "$3B into xAI Series E (**now SpaceX shares**)" — converted.

The dataset's own `modeling_notes` already flagged this: *"Edges should carry: stake type,
confirmed-vs-reported, and vintage."*

**Fixed.** A `relationships` layer records type, status and `as_of` for 20 relationships,
each transcribed verbatim from the record that states it, with the quote and its location
shown in the UI. The remaining 69 edges read **"stake type not recorded"** rather than
implying equity.

### 5. Uncertain assessments presented as definitive winners

Compare marked ▲ best / ▼ worst on every axis with green and red row fills, ignoring
confidence entirely. **55 of 90 assessments carry confidence C** ("thin / inferred /
single-source"), so most "wins" were one guess beating another — Canva's unsourced labour
**3** beating Anthropic's unsourced **2**, both C.

**Fixed.** A difference is only called when **both** sides rest on evidence about that
maker *and* carry confidence A or B. Otherwise the row reads "too little evidence here to
compare these makers", and individual cells that cannot take part are tagged "not ranked".
35 of 90 assessments qualify.

### 6. Internal drafting notes published as methodology

The About page rendered `rubric.md` in full. It is an internal design document, addressed
to the project's author:

- "**v1, design draft**" · "**Status: Design draft for your sign-off**"
- "Two of **your** axes are negatively framed, so **I recommend** renaming"
- "*(your 'exploitation' axis, polarity-flipped)*" · "*(per your decision)*"
- "**§7 Open decisions to confirm before the evidence pass**" — seven questions the live
  site had already answered
- "**§8 Sequenced next steps: 1. You confirm Section 7.**"

Two more leaked into the interface directly: "needs dedicated sourced research **before any
public launch**" appeared on the About page and on every maker's backer section — an
internal to-do shown to visitors of a launched site.

**Fixed.** A written public methodology (`src/data/methodology.md`) now backs `/about`. The
original draft is kept unedited at `/about/working-draft` behind a banner saying what it
is. The pre-launch note is replaced with a user-facing statement of the same limitation.

### 7. Editorial defaults described as the visitor's own preferences

`DEFAULT_LENS` switched on **8 of 9 concerns** for every first-time visitor. The interface
then called that selection theirs, throughout: "Pick which attributes ***you*** count as
concerns" · "**your** capital lens" · "**your** selected concerns are clear" · "Flagged
under **your** lens" · "No concerns flagged under **your current** lens". A visitor who had
never clicked anything was told that Gulf sovereign capital and founder control were their
concerns. A sortable, red-and-green "Capital fit" column ranked all 18 makers on that
basis.

**Fixed.** Every attribute starts off. The lens has three states — nothing chosen, the
**ValueCompass example lens** (offered by name, banner-labelled wherever it is in use), and
the visitor's own once they touch a switch. Capital fit does not appear at all until a lens
is chosen. The old `v1` storage key is cleared on load, because v1 wrote itself to storage
on first mount and so is no evidence that anyone chose anything.

### 8. Also changed

- **Funder `flag` text** ("HIGHEST SOVEREIGN CONCENTRATION", "another Meta concentration
  tentacle") was headed "Why it matters" and read as fact. It is our analysis, and is now
  labelled "ValueCompass assessment — our reading, not a sourced fact".
- **Backer associations**: **12 of 14** funders with recorded associations have no source
  for them, including characterisations like "Subject to scrutiny over Saudi human-rights
  record" and "Thiel is a prominent conservative/Republican political donor". These now
  carry an "Unverified" badge and are described as leads for research.
- **"Larger polygon = a better actor"** (README, polarity legend) is replaced with wording
  that describes performance under the stated rubric and disclaims a verdict.
- **Stake ≠ control ≠ revenue.** Funder lists, the graph legend and the shared-backers
  panel now state that a stake is not voting control, that a connection is not harm, and
  that nothing here traces where subscription money goes.

## Still requiring editorial research

Ranked by how much of the site rests on them.

1. **Attach entity sources, or convert to `n/a`.** 40 assessments cite nothing. This is the
   largest single gap and the one most visible to a reader.
2. **Replace the background-source pattern on labour scores.** 24 assessments cite only
   sector reading. Priority cases where a real source almost certainly exists: OpenAI's
   Kenya/Sama case (scored 1/B on two general articles), Meta's ~49% of Scale AI and the
   Kenyan moderator litigation (scored 1/B, same two articles), Google's rater-pay
   complaints.
3. **Decide what the 21 withheld scores should be.** Each needs either an entity source, or
   re-recording as `n/a` in `makers.json`. Until then they are held, marked, and visible.
4. **Source or remove the 12 unverified funder associations.** Several are politically
   charged characterisations of named living people published without a citation. This
   carries more risk than anything else on the list.
5. **Reconcile the rubric's own worked example.** The rubric cites Midjourney as the case
   for `n/a` on labour ("simply undisclosed"); the dataset scores it 1/B on the
   Disney/Universal and artists' class-action suits. The dataset looks right and the
   example looks stale — but one of them should change.
6. **Re-verify the 2026 records.** `last_freshness_review` is 2026-06-13; several entries
   describe events as pending or expected later in 2026 (DeepSeek's round, the
   Cohere/Aleph Alpha close, Moonshot's H2 IPO). Tool-layer valuations were never
   individually verified — the meta field says so.
7. **Extend relationship detail.** 69 of 89 edges record no stake type. Each one added
   replaces a "stake type not recorded" chip with a fact.

## Needs your review

- **21 withheld display scores** (§1) — the withholding rule is mechanical and re-runnable,
  but which axes it catches is a judgement worth checking. Full list:
  `node scripts/build-evidence.mjs`.
- **3 hand-checked source mismatches** (§3) — `SOURCE_MISMATCH` in
  `scripts/build-evidence.mjs`, one line of reasoning each.
- **No data migration was performed.** `makers.json` and `funders.json` are byte-identical
  to `main`. Everything above is additive, in `src/data/evidence.json`, regenerable from
  the two source files. Deleting it degrades the site to its previous behaviour rather than
  breaking it.
